# 📜 The Lore: "Biosphere Management Group"

> *"We Kill It, So You Don't Have To. (Disclaimer: We Might Also Burn Down Your House.)"*

## 🏢 The World: Corporate Satire
The game takes place in a world dominated by **Biosphere Management Group (BMG)**, a mega-corporation that holds a monopoly on pharmaceuticals and extends into every corner of society — from municipal pest control contracts to school lunch supplements, from hospital supply chains to municipal water treatment. Streets, parks, and product packaging quietly carry BMG's seal; public health advisories circulate with the company's logo and carefully edited statistics. Where BMG's reach can't be bought, it's regulated: licensing, zoning, and municipal waste contracts all favor corporate partners, creating a patchwork of legal entanglement that muffles dissent.

The corporation maintains a polished public face — charity drives, glossy safety campaigns, and "community resilience" grants — while a shadow infrastructure handles the inconvenient fallout: private cleanup crews, secret research wings, and aggressive legal teams that neutralize leaks (literal and figurative). Propaganda blurs the line between pest and threat, normalizing increasingly violent countermeasures in the name of "public safety."

Everyday life feels commodified. Health clinics push company-formulated treatments; vending machines vend "wellness" boosters with fine print you never read. A second economy thrives on salvaged gear, black-market stabilizers, and contractor tips shared over static-filled radio channels. For most citizens the crisis is a strange headline; for contractors it's a day job, a debt ladder, and a slow unraveling of what "protection" really means under corporate rule.

### The "Incident"
Biosphere Management Group acquired Verdantix (formerly "Hyper-Gro Labs"), a scrappy agrochemical startup that promised to end food shortages with a fast-acting growth formula. At first it was a miracle: crops matured faster, yields spiked, and Verdantix's stock soared. To scale that success, BMG prioritized throughput and margin over safety testing — reformulation cycles were shortened, stabilizers were dialed back, and leak-prone production lines were pushed into continuous operation.

Those shortcuts turned a plant-growth compound into a biologically active mutagen. The reagent began integrating with local microbial and insect populations, then crossing unexpected physiological boundaries. Instead of the intended plant uptake, the agent rewrote host biochemistry in ways no one predicted: rapid somatic growth, accelerated reproduction, and emergent biochemical pathways that produced confined, element-like effects (localized combustion, acidic secretions, electrical discharges).

Initial incidents were small and isolated — bloated vermin, strange burns on livestock — but attempts to contain them made things worse. Waste from the production facilities contaminated runoff and compost, and "containment burns" inadvertently spread volatile residues.

The result: household pests—cockroaches, spiders, wasps—mutated into large, adaptive threats and began exhibiting elemental behaviors tied to chemical concentrations in their environment (e.g., grease-saturated roach colonies with incendiary tendencies; sewer-born wasps with corrosive venom). Local governments received quietly edited data packages; communities received outdated safety advisories and mops. The public at large remains mostly unaware — just another weird headline quickly acquired, edited, and filed away by legal.

When the scale became undeniable, BMG spun up **Division 8** — a deniable shadow operation disguised as "independent contractor services." Rather than admit fault and trigger regulatory intervention, the company deployed these "contractors" to "neutralize surplus biomass." Equipped with company gear, armed with experimental "neutralization tools," and bound by iron-clad NDAs, these workers are the human cost of corporate damage control. Their work orders come through encrypted company tablets; their hazard pay barely covers equipment repairs. They are deployed to residential basements and commercial warehouses with the same casualness as scheduling a plumber.

### The Tone
*   **Cynical & Bureaucratic:** You are risking your life for minimum wage and a chance at a "Performance Bonus."
*   **Darkly Comedic:** The horror of a giant spider eating a cat is juxtaposed with a chirpy HR voice over the radio reminding you to "Protect Company Assets."
*   **Blue Collar:** The aesthetic is industrial—jumpsuits, duct tape, retro-tech scanners, and clipboards.

## 👷 The Exterminators (Division 8 Personnel Files)

You play as "contractors"—desperate individuals who owe debt to the company, are running from something worse, or are simply unhinged enough to see this as an opportunity. BMG doesn't ask questions during hiring; they just need warm bodies willing to sign the waiver.

### 1. The Pyrotechnic ("The Firebug")
> *"I love the smell of napalm in the morning. It smells like... overtime."*

*   **Real Name:** Marcus "Torch" Ramirez
*   **Previous Employment:** Celebrity BBQ chef, competitive pit master, insurance fraud suspect
*   **Hired Because:** Outstanding warrant for "reckless endangerment with grilling equipment." BMG offered to clear his record in exchange for "extended field service."
*   **Backstory:** Marcus was a rising star in the competitive BBQ circuit until an "accident" at the Regional Finals left three judges with second-degree burns and a pavilion reduced to ash. His defense—"The meat was perfect though"—didn't hold up in court. When BMG's recruitment officer visited his holding cell with a contract and a lighter, Marcus didn't hesitate. He sees the mutant bugs as the ultimate culinary challenge, though the company's "Economy Grade" fuel (which leaks constantly) means he's usually dodging his own flames.
*   **Field Notes:** Subject exhibits concerning enthusiasm when setting things on fire. Recommend reassignment if he starts naming the flames.
*   **Mechanic:** **Infinite Fire Spread / Self-Immolation Risk.**
    *   *The Power:* His flames don't just burn; they spread like a living thing, clinging to enemies and surfaces alike. Fire chains between targets, creating cascading infernos.
    *   *The Constraint:* The fuel tank leaks behind him as he moves. Standing still for more than a second causes ignition beneath his feet. He must keep moving forward—always forward—into the fire he creates.

### 2. The Apiarist ("The Beekeeper")
> *"They don't pay me enough to touch the bugs myself."*

*   **Real Name:** Beatrix "Queen B" Vance
*   **Previous Employment:** Urban Beekeeper, Etsy "Power Seller," Unlicensed Bio-Hazard Handler
*   **Hired Because:** Her "organic, small-batch" honey was found to be a Class-3 Neurotoxin. BMG acquired her stock (for weapons R&D) and her silence (with a job offer).
*   **Backstory:** Beatrix was selling "Hyper-Local Honey" at farmer's markets for $40 a jar before the Incident. Her customers loved the "tingly mouthfeel." Turns out, her bees were foraging on Verdantix runoff flowers. When the bees grew to fist-size and started defending the hive with lethal force, Beatrix didn't call exterminators—she realized she had a private security force. She manages the swarm with a mix of pheromones and stern talking-to. She joined Division 8 because "bees need protein, and there's a lot of spare biomass walking around."
*   **Field Notes:** Subject refers to the swarm as "my babies." Do not accept honey samples.
*   **Mechanic:** **Swarm Management / Bio-Feedback.**
    *   *The Power:* "The Hive." Killing marked enemies spawns Ghost Bees that attack nearby threats. The swarm grows with every kill.
    *   *The Constraint:* "Shared Fate." Her health is tied to her swarm. Taking damage kills bees instead of HP; if she runs out of bees, she takes direct damage. She must farm kills to maintain her living shield.

### 3. The Custodian ("The Janitor")
> *"Clean up on Aisle 4. And 5. And everywhere."*

*   **Real Name:** Evelyn "Eve" Ortega
*   **Previous Employment:** Head custodian at Clearwater High School (22 years of service)
*   **Hired Because:** School budget cuts eliminated her pension. BMG offered "contractor rates with performance bonuses"—which beat her retirement plan.
*   **Backstory:** Eve spent two decades mopping floors, scraping gum, and maintaining order in a building full of chaos. She knew every corner of that school, every stain, every surface. When the district axed her pension to fund a new football scoreboard, she walked out with her mop and her dignity. BMG's recruitment pitch was simple: "You've cleaned up worse messes. These ones just fight back." Eve agreed, figuring giant bugs couldn't be worse than teenagers. She was wrong, but the pay is better. Her decades of experience give her an almost supernatural sense of spacing—she knows exactly how far her mop reaches and exactly where enemies need to be.
*   **Field Notes:** Subject demonstrates exceptional spatial awareness. Refuses to use "inferior cleaning equipment." Brings her own mop.
*   **Mechanic:** **Precision Spacing / Minimum Range Weakness.**
    *   *The Power:* The mop has extended reach. Hitting enemies at maximum range (the outer arc of her swing) deals triple damage and launches them into walls for devastating "splat" bonuses.
    *   *The Constraint:* Enemies inside her minimum range (within 50% of mop reach) take zero damage. She must control spacing constantly or become helpless. A danger indicator shows when enemies breach her guard.

### 4. The Intern ("The Heavy")
> *"My back hurts and I'm not even getting paid."*

*   **Real Name:** Jamie Park
*   **Previous Employment:** Unpaid intern at six different companies (simultaneously)
*   **Hired Because:** Desperate for literally any job that pays. Saw "entry-level position, no experience necessary" and didn't read the fine print.
*   **Backstory:** Jamie graduated with honors and $140,000 in student debt. After two years of unpaid internships, coffee runs, and "exposure," they were ready to take any job that offered actual money. When BMG's job posting said "Heavy Equipment Operator — Will Train," Jamie thought it meant forklifts. It did not. The "industrial foam sprayer" weighs 60 pounds empty and leaks hardening foam that coats everything—including the operator. Jamie's too tired and too broke to quit, so they've learned to weaponize exhaustion. The heavier the armor, the less they care about dodging.
*   **Field Notes:** Subject shows concerning lack of self-preservation instinct. Possibly too overworked to feel fear. Recommend monitoring for burnout (and literal burn damage).
*   **Mechanic:** **Ablative Armor / Mobility Trade-off.**
    *   *The Power:* The foam sprayer coats Jamie in hardening foam, building up to 200 points of damage-absorbing armor. The foam also slows and damages enemies caught in the spray.
    *   *The Constraint:* Movement speed decreases as armor increases (100 armor = 50% slower movement). At max armor, Jamie is a slow juggernaut. Taking damage removes armor and speeds them up, creating a constant oscillation between fast/fragile and slow/tanky.

### 5. The Electrician ("Sparky")
> *"It's not a safety violation if it works."*

*   **Real Name:** Victor "Vic" Kowalski
*   **Previous Employment:** Licensed electrician, union rep, OSHA whistleblower
*   **Hired Because:** Blacklisted from legitimate work after reporting too many safety violations. BMG doesn't care about OSHA complaints.
*   **Backstory:** Vic was a by-the-book union electrician who believed in doing things the right way—until he reported his employer for running live wires through active water pipes. His reward was being blacklisted across the entire industry. When BMG offered him a contractor position with "flexible safety standards," Vic was suspicious but broke. The jumper cables they issued him are a fire marshal's nightmare: exposed wiring, no breakers, no shutoff switch. Just raw, arcing death. Vic has made peace with the irony—he's now the safety violation he used to report. But the bugs don't file complaints, and the paychecks clear.
*   **Field Notes:** Subject frequently mutters "This violates at least six codes" while using equipment. Recommend confiscating his OSHA handbook.
*   **Mechanic:** **Chain Lightning / Thermal Overload.**
    *   *The Power:* Chain lightning arcs between enemies within range. Each additional arc increases damage by 25%. Killing an enemy in a chain causes an electrical explosion.
    *   *The Constraint:* No ammo limit, but the weapon builds heat with use. At 100% heat, the weapon force-discharges, dealing 50% of max HP as self-damage and stunning Vic for one second. He must manage heat carefully or risk catastrophic failure.

### 6. The Scientist ("Dr. Overkill")
> *"The pest control is experimental. The lawsuits are real."*

*   **Real Name:** Dr. Sarah Chen, Ph.D.
*   **Previous Employment:** Senior researcher at Verdantix Labs (fired post-Incident)
*   **Hired Because:** She knows what BMG did. They hired her to keep her quiet.
*   **Backstory:** Dr. Chen was the lead biochemist on the Hyper-Gro project. She raised concerns about the accelerated testing schedule, the missing stabilizer trials, and the anomalous readings in the arthropod exposure groups. Her reports were filed, then buried. When the mutations started, she tried to go public. BMG's legal team arrived first with a termination notice, an NDA, and a choice: accept a "contractor position" with Division 8 or face charges for "intellectual property theft." Now she's in the field with a prototype particle beam that was never meant to leave the lab—a weapon built from the same destabilized compounds that created this mess. She tells herself she's gathering data. Really, she's trying to survive long enough to find proof.
*   **Field Notes:** Subject exhibits signs of guilt and extreme competence. Monitor for whistleblower tendencies. Approved for "experimental equipment"—losses are acceptable.
*   **Mechanic:** **Charge Laser / Critical Instability.**
    *   *The Power:* Fully charged shots pierce through all enemies and walls, dealing massive damage. It's the most powerful weapon in Division 8's arsenal.
    *   *The Constraint:* Charging above 75% has a 5% chance per second to cause critical failure—dealing 30% max HP as self-damage and stunning her for one second. The longer she charges, the more she gambles. Uncharged shots do minimal damage, forcing risk-reward decisions.

## 🗺️ The Contract System (Progression)

Instead of a single continuous journey, your career at Biosphere Management Group is defined by **Contracts**—legally binding work orders delivered through your company-issued tablet. You choose a deployment zone from the Mission Board in the Company Van (a repurposed delivery truck with suspicious stains and a broken radio). Each contract comes with a hazard assessment, liability waiver, and reminder that "equipment damage will be deducted from final compensation."

Completing a contract unlocks "Overtime Mode" (Endless) for that specific zone—because BMG's definition of "work-life balance" is "work until you drop, then work some more."

### Current Deployment Zones

#### **Contract #001: Suburbia Clean-Up (Launch Map)**
*   **Setting:** Ruined living rooms, overgrown backyards, infested garages.
*   **The Mission:** Clear 25 rooms of Class 1 and Class 2 biomass.
*   **The Boss:** **The Queen Roach** (Sector 7G).
*   **Endless Unlock:** "Overtime in the Burbs" — Fight until the union forces a break (or you die).

#### **Future Contracts (Coming Soon)**
*   **Contract #002: Municipal Waste Facility (The Sewers)**
    *   *Hazard:* Toxic sludge and corrosive leaks.
*   **Contract #003: R&D Facility Omega (The Lab)**
    *   *Hazard:* Reality-warping breaches and experimental escapees.

### The "Upgrades"
You don't find magical artifacts. You find:
*   **"Stolen Office Supplies"** (+Speed)
*   **"Unauthorized Modifications"** (+Damage)
*   **"Union Membership Card"** (+Defense)
*   **"Performance Review"** (Level Up)
